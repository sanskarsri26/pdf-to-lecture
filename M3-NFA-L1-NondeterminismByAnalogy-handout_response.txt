**Slide 3: Navigating a Maze (Determinism)**

**Slide Title:** Navigating a Maze: Determinism

**Detailed Explanation:** Let's start with a deterministic approach to maze navigation.  A deterministic strategy guarantees a specific outcome given a set of instructions.  For example, "always take the leftmost path" is a deterministic strategy.  While it might work for some mazes, it's not universally successful.  Determinism focuses on what *must* happen based on a pre-defined rule. The success or failure is directly tied to the chosen strategy.


**Slide 4: Navigating a Maze (Nondeterminism)**

**Slide Title:** Navigating a Maze: Nondeterminism

**Detailed Explanation:** Nondeterminism, conversely, examines *all possible* paths simultaneously.  It doesn't focus on a specific strategy but on the inherent properties of the maze itself. Can the maze be solved at all? A nondeterministic approach would explore every possible path to determine solvability, irrespective of a specific navigation strategy.  This helps us understand the maze’s properties, not just our ability to navigate it with a chosen algorithm.


**Slide 5: Routing Packets on the Internet**

**Slide Title:** Routing Packets on the Internet

**Detailed Explanation:** Consider sending a data packet across the internet.  The route it takes is nondeterministic. Numerous paths exist between your computer and the server. The actual path is chosen dynamically by routers based on network conditions like congestion and link availability.  Nondeterminism allows us to model this inherent uncertainty in network routing. We plan for multiple possibilities rather than assuming a specific route.


**Slide 6: Planning Moves in Chess**

**Slide Title:** Planning Moves in Chess

**Detailed Explanation:**  In chess, planning involves anticipating your opponent's moves.  Your strategy cannot be purely deterministic, since your opponent's choices are unknown.  Nondeterministic game-tree search explores all possible opponent responses to each of your moves. This allows for planning that accounts for various possibilities, crucial for optimal strategic decision-making in adversarial scenarios.


**Slide 7: Why Nondeterminism?**

**Slide Title:** Why Nondeterminism?

**Detailed Explanation:** Nondeterminism is crucial in theoretical computer science.  It provides the *power* to explore multiple solutions in parallel, potentially overcoming limitations of deterministic algorithms.  Moreover, it enables modeling *uncertainty*: considering all possibilities can lead to robust guarantees concerning the correctness, security, and performance of a system. The existence of true nondeterminism in the real world is a complex philosophical question, beyond the immediate scope of this lecture.
